Nvidia builds the fastest AI GPUs on earth, yet thirty-thousand-dollar racks still wait on tiny vertical memory cubes from South Korea's SK Hynix. Even crazier: rival memory giant Samsung helped pioneer HBM — then disbanded its core HBM team in 2019 right before AI changed computing forever.

Here is the 13-year story of how AMD's 2013 skyscraper gamble, liquid MR-MUF underfill, and TSMC CoWoS turned SK Hynix into AI's kingmaker — and why every gigabyte of HBM eats 3x the silicon wafer of normal RAM.

You'll learn:
- Why AMD & SK Hynix built the first 3D-stacked HBM chip in 2013 — and why Samsung walked away in 2019
- The Memory Wall: why 10,000 GPU cores starve behind a 64-bit bus vs. HBM's 1,024-bit vertical TSV elevators
- The Glue War: how SK Hynix's liquid MR-MUF beat TC-NCF film on 12-layer HBM3E heat and yields
- The 3x Wafer Tradeoff: how HBM cannibalized DDR5 capacity and why HBM4 binds SK Hynix to TSMC
